# Good Faith program: internal security review

This is an internal review. An independent audit is recommended before any mainnet deployment.

## Scope
`programs/goodfaith` (Anchor 1.2.1), all thirty-two instructions (v5: bars kept per role with the borrower's own pay-back the only thing that relaxes theirs, settlement that applies only the opening deposit, escrows that always close whatever is sent to them, restitution that releases an uncollected recovery instead of paying from the vault, claims that wait out a wallet move, a cap reset at settlement, `forgive`, and floors on the administrator (a minimum recovery delay, a quarantine after identity-key changes, a two-key successor); v4: a closed loan state machine with no repayment after the deadline, settlement as bookkeeping with entitlements collected later, restitution with bar relaxation, snapshotted loan terms, seasoned caps, an outstanding-principal ceiling and a public bar lift, on top of v3's opaque person ids, guarded two-step wallet recovery, time-bounded bars, pull-based claims, request expiry, exposure and disbursement limits, emergency pause and minimum notice period, and v2's person-level eligibility, timelocked settings, wind-down and sweep), `gateway.rs`, checked against `docs/spec.md`. The `app/` directory is out of scope except where a finding names it (GF-09, GF-10, GF-19).

## Method and tools
- `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` (clippy 0.1.99): clean.
- `cargo audit` 0.22.2: no vulnerabilities. Five "unmaintained" warnings in transitive dependencies (ansi_term, bincode 1.3.3, derivative, libsecp256k1, paste), all pulled in by the Solana and Anchor stacks.
- Manual line-by-line review of every instruction: signers, owners, PDA seeds and stored bumps, account substitution, duplicate mutable accounts, arithmetic, close handling, permissionless paths, stale state.
- Civic gateway layout re-checked against `identity-com/on-chain-identity-gateway` (`solana/program/src/state.rs`).
- Tests added after the review (70 total in v3, including the v2 paths: issuer-signed registration, one person one wallet in both directions, wallet replacement with escrow refunds to the new wallet on repay and default, retired wallets, the default policy both ways, the timelock lifecycle including the timelock changing itself and freeze discarding a pending change, wind-down gates, cancellation window, successor validation and sweep, a frozen pool keeping an announced wind-down, issuer suspension and restoration, the `active_loans` counter): the error paths the first pass left to `is_err` are now asserted by code, including `LoanNotActive` (reached by naming a live token account as the closed escrow, since Anchor deserialises before it checks constraints), account substitution (rent recipients, token owners, vault) by Anchor code, initialise/register twice, a member PDA from another pool, zero-escrow default, late repayment before default, and malformed gateway bytes.
- Property tests: Trident (stable 0.12.0, 0.13.0-rc.4 pre-release) was not attempted. It needs its own harness and fuzz workspace and its Anchor 1.2.1 support is unconfirmed, so I wrote proptest 1.x property tests over LiteSVM 0.18.1 instead (`props::invariants_hold`: 24 random sequences of up to 40 operations locally and 200 in CI (`PROPTEST_CASES`), over a pool with a three-day timelock, including scheduled and applied settings that vary the ban durations and seasoning, cancellations, wallet replacements, wind-down announcements and cancellations, sweeps, issuer suspension, escrow top-ups, freezes and thaws, lifts, forgiveness and clock jumps). The count is stated as it is: tens of sequences by default, hundreds in CI, not thousands.

### Reviewed with no issue found
- Signer, owner and `has_one`-style checks: every authority path is a `Signer` plus a key constraint (`pool.authority`, `pool.pending_authority`, `request.cosigner`, `request.borrower`, `loan.*`). `freeze_pool` sets `authority = None` and `pending_authority = None`; `accept_authority` requires a pending key, so nothing can set an authority on a frozen pool: freezing is irreversible.
- PDA seeds use the canonical bump stored at creation (`pool.bump`, `loan.bump`, `loan.escrow_bump`, `member.bump`, `request.bump`).
- Account substitution: vault is pinned to `pool.vault`; escrow is a PDA of the loan; every token account is constrained by mint and authority; rent recipients are pinned to the stored `rent_payer`; the mint account must match the pool PDA seeds; token program is `Interface<TokenInterface>` (only the two real token programs).
- Duplicate accounts: borrower and co-signer are different people (the request checks `cosigner_member.person != borrower_person`, the co-sign checks the two Person keys); `payer_token` cannot be the vault or escrow (their authority is the pool PDA, which cannot sign).
- Person and Member: both are `init` at registration, so an id cannot get a second wallet and a wallet cannot get a second person; Members are never closed and `finalize_rebind` creates the new Member with `init`, so a retired wallet can never come back under any person. Persons are self-seeded by their stored id and pinned to the wallet that signs (request, cosign) or to the loan (repay, default, claim), so no Person can be substituted. The escrow claim follows `cosigner_person.wallet`, never the stale `loan.cosigner`; `claim_escrow` checks the token account's owner and mint.
- Recovery: `propose_rebind` needs the issuer's and the recovery authority's signatures and a pending slot that is empty, and the new wallet's gateway token must be valid (the new wallet itself does not sign, so a proposal cannot name a wallet the identity service never checked); `veto_rebind` is open to exactly the old wallet and the authority; `finalize_rebind` enforces the delay and the pending wallet, and a second proposal while one is pending is refused, so a recovery can neither be rushed nor hijacked mid-way. A retired wallet cannot veto (it is no longer `person.wallet`).
- `lift_bar`: authority only, requires a live bar, clears the two role bars and nothing else (no cap, loan, entitlement or loss count is touched); it cannot set a bar. `relax_borrower_bar` acts only when the loan paying back is the one that set the bar and only lowers it, so a later default's bar or a lift is never shortened by a restitution; the co-signer bar answers to the loss count alone.
- Settlement ordering: `repay` in full (only up to the deadline) and `settle` (only after it) are the only transitions out of Active, so the two cannot race; both run the same guarantee and counter decrements; settlement applies at most the opening deposit and records the rest of the balance as the co-signer's. `collect_recovery` and `claim_escrow` move the recorded amounts (plus any stray balance to its rightful side) and zero them; each refuses when nothing is pending, and the escrow is closed exactly when it is empty. `restitute` is bounded by `shortfall − restituted`, orders the fund's loss before the co-signer's share by arithmetic on stored fields (no per-payment state), releases uncollected escrow before touching the vault, and flips to `Restituted` exactly at equality (or at `forgive`). The claim's escrow account is optional so a closed or frozen escrow never blocks the vault part; the vault part is bounded by `vault_claims`, which the sweep leaves behind and `cosign_loan` never lends.
- Caps: the personal cap is computed from the person's own record and the live pool settings at request and again at co-sign; exposure is checked for both people at co-sign from their current `active_guarantee_amount` and the borrower's would-be principal; the disbursement window rolls before the cap is checked, with checked math; the reciprocity check needs the co-signer's open loan account and refuses a substitute (`MissingCosignerLoan` pins the key).
- Timelock: `schedule_params` validates, `apply_params` validates again before applying; `params_effective_at` uses the timelock in force at scheduling; `freeze_pool` discards pending settings; `apply_params` has no signer so the operator's cranker or anyone can apply, which only ever enacts what the authority announced.
- Wind-down: the gate is `now >= wind_down_at`; `announce` refuses a second announcement, `cancel` refuses once in effect, `sweep` requires effect, `active_loans == 0` and the exact successor. `active_loans` is incremented at cosign and decremented at repay-in-full and default (checked math, never below zero).
- Arithmetic: checked everywhere; the escrow ceiling is computed in u128 and is at most the loan amount because bps <= 10000.
- Close handling: loan accounts are never closed, loan ids never repeat, so escrows cannot be revived. Request accounts use Anchor `close`. Pre-funding a loan or escrow PDA with lamports, or sending extra tokens to an escrow at any point, does not block cosign, repay, settlement, collection or claim (extra tokens go to the co-signer at a claim or to the vault as a donation at the collection that empties the account), and the empty account always closes, frozen or not.
- Permissionless calls: third-party repay and `mark_default` only move funds along the spec'd paths. Tiny repayments cost the caller only.
- Stale `LoanRequest`: eligibility, gateway tokens, limits, vault balance and active-loan state are re-checked at cosign time.

### Civic gateway (`gateway.rs`)
Verified against upstream: `GatewayToken` is Borsh with no discriminator, in this order: `version u8`, `parent_gateway_token Option<Pubkey>`, `owner_wallet`, `owner_identity Option<Pubkey>`, `gatekeeper_network`, `issuing_gatekeeper`, `state` (1 byte: Active=0, Frozen=1, Revoked=2), `expire_time Option<i64>`. Options use a 0/1 tag byte. `version` is always 0 and unused upstream. Expiry is only in `expire_time`; there are no other feature flags in the account. Token address derivation upstream is `find_program_address([owner, "gateway", 8-byte additional seed (zeros by default), network], gateway program)`. The program id in `gateway.rs` is the Civic one. The code does not recompute the PDA: it requires the account owner to be the gateway program and checks `owner_wallet` and `gatekeeper_network` from the data, which is equivalent to upstream's check and also accepts tokens issued with a non-zero seed. Trailing bytes are ignored. One small difference: upstream treats a token as valid when `now == expire_time`; this program requires `expiry > now` (stricter by one second, matching the spec). I could not fetch the integration crate source to re-confirm its exact check order, so that part rests on the account layout above.

## Invariants tested (`props::invariants_hold`, checked after every operation)
1. Vault balance == total_donated + total_repaid + total_recovered + total_restituted - total_lent - total_swept - total_reimbursed.
2. Token conservation: vault + escrows + every wallet that ever held tokens (current and retired) + the successor equal the supply handed out.
3. For every loan, repaid <= principal; Repaid loans have repaid == principal; the borrower and co-signer persons differ.
4. While a loan is Active its escrow account exists and holds exactly `loan.escrowed`; closed loans have no open escrow; `pool.active_loans` equals the number of Active loans.
5. Per person: `active_guarantees` equals the number of Active loans they co-signed (never underflows); `active_loan` matches the single Active loan they borrowed, and no person has two; `person.wallet` is the actor's current wallet, whose Member is not retired and points at that person; every replaced wallet's Member is retired.
6. Settings only change through `apply_params` (the live settings always equal the last applied `PoolParams`, with `suspend_issuer` zeroing the issuer); a pending change always validates.
7. Once a wind-down is in effect, `loan_count` and `total_donated` never move again and the state never leaves wind-down; a sweep only happens with no Active loan.
8. Settlement policy, per role: every Defaulted loan records `borrower_bar_until = closed_at + default_ban_seconds` (its own snapshot) and, with a loss, `cosigner_bar_until = closed_at + cosigner_ban_seconds`; a Restituted loan's borrower bar is either that or a late bar between `closed_at + late_ban_seconds` and `now + late_ban_seconds`; each person's `borrower_bar_until` and `cosigner_bar_until` are at least every such bar set since their last lift, `ineligible_until` is exactly their maximum, and a person with no loss outstanding has no co-signer bar. A bar is never shortened except by the borrower's own `restitute` (by rule) or `lift_bar`.
9. Entitlements: a settled loan's escrow account holds at least `refund_pending + recovery_pending` (or is closed when both are 0 and the account is empty); a Repaid loan's at least `refund_pending`; an Active loan's at least `escrowed`; `recovery_pending ≤ recovered`; `pool.pending_claims` = Σ `refund_pending`, `pool.recoveries_pending` = Σ `recovery_pending`, `pool.vault_claims` = Σ `reimbursement_pending`; the vault holds at least `vault_claims`; the conservation equation (2) counts every escrow and every stray deposit.
10. Caps: per person, principal borrowed plus `active_guarantee_amount` ≤ `max_exposure_per_person`; `active_guarantee_amount` equals the principal of the Active loans they co-sign; no two Active loans guarantee each other's borrower; `completed_loans` equals the on-time repayments of loans held at least `seasoning_seconds`; `window_disbursed` ≤ the cap when one is set; `pool.outstanding_principal` = Σ (principal − repaid) over Active loans ≤ `max_outstanding_principal`.
11. Pause: while paused no loan opens; `loan_count` only moves while lending is open.
12. State machine: no loan is repaid after its deadline; every settled loan was settled after it; `shortfall = principal − repaid`, `recovered = min(shortfall, escrowed)` whatever was sent to the escrow since, `restituted ≤ shortfall`, `Restituted ⇔ restituted == shortfall or forgiven`, `reimbursement_pending ≤ restituted − lost`; every loan carries the ban durations, grace period, seasoning and step interval the pool had at opening (the model snapshots the settings as each loan opens); `completed_loans` counts seasoned on-time repayments since the person's last settlement; `unrestituted_losses` equals the number of settled loans with a loss the person co-signed whose loss the fund has not had back.

The property test (`props::invariants_hold`, random sequences including claims, top-ups into escrows, freezes and thaws, lifts, forgiveness, pause and resume, recovery proposals, vetoes and finalisations, expired-request closes, settings that vary the ban durations and seasoning, and clock jumps across deadlines) assumes no tokens are sent straight to the vault; that is covered by a separate unit test.

## Residual risks
- GF-05 and GF-06 above.
- The authority is trusted within the timelock (GF-07, GF-34): every change is visible for `timelock_seconds` before it applies, and anyone may then apply it; a change to the identity keys is followed by a seven-day quarantine on wallet moves, the recovery delay never drops below a day, and the successor of a wind-down needs a second key. Within those floors the administrator can still replace the identity keys with notice. Freezing stays optional; a wind-down needs a live authority and the successor authority to announce.
- Sybil resistance is only as strong as the issuer service's identity check and its credential fingerprints (GF-10, GF-13, GF-20); the program enforces one wallet per person id, not one id per human. The money a Sybil ring can extract is bounded by the caps in GF-16 and the pause in GF-17, not eliminated.
- The successor named in a wind-down is trusted (GF-12).
- A co-signer and borrower who collude can walk away with the loan minus the escrow; the first-loan cap, the exposure limit and the time-bounded bars limit how much and how often, and the escrow is a deterrent, not full cover.
- The recovery delay protects a member only if they notice the proposal: the app shows a pending recovery to the old wallet, a notice goes to the contact the member gave, and the operator reviews every request before signing with a key the web service does not hold (`docs/identity.md`); a member who gave no contact and never looks during the delay has only that review.
- The program is upgradeable during the pilot: whoever holds the upgrade authority can change the code, which overrides every other guarantee here. That authority is a separate multisig behind a published delay with published exit criteria (`docs/operations.md`), and the paper states the arrangement rather than claiming immutability.
- A frozen escrow or vault delays money until it thaws; the program records who is owed what and never writes it off, but cannot move a frozen account (GF-06, GF-22).
- The fraud figures in `docs/economics.md` are scenarios under stated containment times; the program bounds the per-window, per-person and total exposure, not how fast an operator notices.
- Direct token transfers into the vault are not counted in `total_donated`.
- Dependencies carry unmaintained-crate warnings (see above); no known vulnerabilities at review time.
- Not fuzzed with Trident or coverage-guided tools; the property test is bounded.
